AZANI · DESIGN REVIEW · 17 SEPTEMBER 2026

A warm kids clothing boutique, with a clearer shopping experience.

Verdict: Azani has a recognisable, welcoming direction. The next gain comes from merchandise quality, fit confidence, clearer controls and a disciplined visual system—not adding more decoration.

Benchmark: Kiabi UAE baby collection. Borrow its product emphasis, image consistency and browsing patterns. Keep Azani’s ages 2–12 focus, colour identity and explicit size selection. Avoid its intrusive signup popup and broad department structure.

Scope and limits

Current-run screenshots and DOM inspection of homepage, collection, product/fit guidance, cart, checkout address, phone navigation/filter states, empty Sale and login. Kiabi desktop collection, drawer, product and a 919px tablet-width view were captured. A Kiabi phone-width capture was not obtained; do not treat the comparison as a complete mobile benchmark. Azani phone width measured 388px. Viewport sizes varied; comparisons concern hierarchy and behaviour, not pixel-identical geometry.

No storefront code or product data changed during this audit. No sign-in, payment or order submission was performed. Fixture assortment limits judgement of real catalogue depth. Performance, full contrast compliance, screen-reader behaviour and later checkout stages remain unverified.

Recommended order of work

1. Fix buying confidence

Labels and control names, reliable size data, accurate delivery/totals, mobile drawer controls and honest empty states.

2. Refine the shopping shell

Compact catalogue, visible search, photo category rail, coherent filters, quiet cards and a clearer product purchase panel.

3. Apply the design system

Explicit corner, icon, typography, spacing and colour rules; original imagery and restrained homepage merchandising.

4. Validate release quality

Realistic catalogue, phone/keyboard journeys, long content, failed/empty states, accessibility and production performance.

Complete prioritised findings

P0: resolve before a public shopping launch. P1: next iteration. P2: visual polish and consistency. Verify: additional evidence required. Proposed dimensions are starting design rules, not claims of measured defects.

P1 · 21Checkout focus

P1 · 22Form visibility

P1 · 23Product identity in checkout

P2 · 24Visual direction
Steps 03–10		Azani is friendly, but repeated pastel containers make utility screens feel more decorative than needed.Aim for a warm kids clothing boutique: calm shopping surfaces, joyful photography and small brand accents. Ensure older children aged 9–12 are represented, not just toddler aesthetics.
P2 · 25Colour roles
Steps 03–10		Pink, blue, yellow, green and slate currently serve both decoration and utility.Keep warm ivory/white as the base, slate for text, raspberry for the primary action. Use pale blue/blush/butter for editorial sections; reserve green for success and red for errors.
P2 · 26Corner system
Steps 03–10,17		Pill inputs, pill links, rounded cards, large panels and the hero arch share attention without a clear hierarchy.Proposed tokens: 8px inputs and image corners; 12px cards/dialogs; 20px editorial tiles; full pills only for chips and primary brand CTAs. P2 · 27Icon family
Steps 03,10,14		Navigation uses a dress/shirt, category filters use sparkles/cloud, and utilities use another size/emphasis.Use one stroke family, consistent optical size and 1.5–2px strokes. P2 · 28Icon meaning
Steps 03,14		A dress stands for all girls’ clothing and a shirt for all boys’ clothing.Retain readable labels; treat icons as decorative rather than exclusive category definitions. P2 · 29Typography hierarchy
Steps 03–10		Friendly headings work; secondary labels, stock text and utility copy become small and faint.Keep Nunito for editorial headings and Geist for shopping/form text. Proposed scale: 14–16px body, 12px minimum supporting metadata, 28–36px section headings, 40–56px hero depending on viewport. Test real long names.
P2 · 30Spacing rhythm
Steps 03–10		Large empty areas coexist with tightly packed metadata and tiny links.Use a 4/8px spacing system: 16px mobile page gutters, 24–32px desktop gutters, 12–16px grid gaps, 32–56px section spacing. Give related controls proximity; avoid adding a box to create grouping.
P2 · 31Product-card shape
Steps 03 versus01		Whole-card borders and rounded bottoms make the grid look like a dashboard of widgets.Explore borderless product cards with a softly rounded image only, aligned text and stable card heights. Preserve the 3:4 garment image ratio.
P2 · 32Homepage hero
Steps 10–11		A single flat-lay shirt is enlarged into the main campaign image and dominates the first mobile viewport.Use an original lifestyle image representing the target age range, supported by clear garment photos. Shorten the mobile hero so an age/category entry appears sooner. Keep one primary CTA.
P2 · 33Photo colour fidelity
Steps 10		Category images are tinted by their pastel tile treatment.Keep garment colours accurate against a consistent neutral background; apply colour around the image rather than through it.
P2 · 34Homepage assortment
Steps 10		The same small fixture assortment supplies hero, categories and product cards.Merchandise a coherent, varied demo or real collection. Avoid repeating the same three garments in every section; do not invent bestsellers or reviews.
P2 · 35Header proportions
Steps 03,10,14		The icon nav is friendly but search and account controls are visually secondary and compact.Keep icon-plus-label navigation, an obvious current destination and generous click targets. Test desktop just above the mobile breakpoint and long labels. Consider a more visible search entry before adding more nav destinations.
P2 · 36Footer hierarchy
Steps 06,08,17		Large repeated navigation lists include Checkout as a general link and placeholder-looking contact details.Use Shop, Help and About groups; remove general Checkout navigation. Publish verified support contacts, response hours, delivery/returns and size-help links. Collapse groups on mobile.
P2 · 37Promo-code emphasis
Steps 06		Promo code occupies its own large panel between totals and the checkout action.Collapse it behind “Have a promo code?” so checkout remains the dominant next step. Keep feedback inline when expanded.
P2 · 38Copy consistency
Steps 03–17		The site mixes “Shop All”, “All Clothing”, “Browse”, “New In” and “New arrivals”; sizes are plain numbers.Choose consistent task labels and sentence case. Explain the real size system, e.g. years or supplier sizes, without inferring units from a number. Simplify “1 item (1 units)”.
P2 · 39Motion
Steps 10,12–17		The promotional text rotates and the help control has an attention-catching halo.Use restrained motion, honour reduced-motion preferences and avoid animated attention cues beside purchase controls.
P2 · 40Account entry
Steps 17		The form is straightforward, but borders are faint and the full footer follows immediately.Keep sign-in compact, improve field/focus visibility, and make the password reveal control clearly labelled. Verify Google sign-in separately before featuring it as a primary route.
P2 · 41Price formatting
Steps 03–08		Prices consistently include .00 and sit beside small metadata.Choose one local-currency display convention; consider whole-shilling display for whole prices while preserving exact values where needed. Keep price prominent and sale maths unambiguous.
P2 · 42Reassurance precision
Steps 04,06,08		Delivery and return reassurance is repeated but not always specific to the order.Show a short accurate returns summary and delivery estimate at the decision point; link to detail rather than repeat generic promises.
Verify · 43Interaction quality
Steps All		This pass captured selected flows, not every input/error/loading state.Test search suggestions, empty wishlist/cart, keyboard focus, Back/Forward restoration, filter combinations, image failures, loading shifts, validation and payment recovery before claiming release readiness.
Verify · 44Performance and accessibility
Steps All		No production performance run, complete screen-reader audit or measured contrast inventory was conducted.Set image budgets, responsive image sizes and layout-stability targets; measure contrast and focus; test 200% zoom, 320px widths, touch targets and reduced motion on real devices.

Evidence walkthrough

STEP 01 · Strong product focus

Kiabi desktop collection

Photo category rail, restrained image corners, consistent product photography and immediately reachable search. Do not copy its adult/baby taxonomy into an ages 2–12 store.

STEP 02 · Useful reference

Kiabi filter drawer

Grouped filter controls and a persistent Apply action. Sorting takes substantial vertical space; Azani can be more concise.

STEP 03 · Needs refinement

Azani collection

Readable navigation and garment imagery, but large title/sidebar surfaces reduce browsing space. Only two matching fixtures were present.

STEP 04 · Needs stronger buying guidance

Azani product

Clear option sequence and honest sold-out state; single image, QA content and very faint controls.

STEP 05 · Launch blocker for confident fit

Azani size guide

Measurement values are not supplied. This is a data problem as well as a presentation problem.

STEP 06 · Usable; clarity work needed

Azani cart

Prominent checkout action. Quantity/header mismatch, unnamed controls and an unconditional delivery estimate need attention. Existing local cart was inspected; no order was created.

STEP 07 · Avoid copying this interruption

Kiabi product popup

Signup overlay obscures product selection. Captured intentionally as a negative benchmark example.

STEP 08 · Accessibility fixes required

Azani checkout address

Clear progression but weak input boundaries and missing label associations confirmed in the DOM. Later payment/review steps were not completed.

STEP 09 · Useful merchandise hierarchy

Kiabi product after dismissal

Large photo, strong price and purchase action, size guide and material/care sections. The observed default size should not override Azani’s explicit size-choice policy.

STEP 10 · Good personality; needs merchandising

Azani homepage

Warm palette and distinctive heading. Repeated fixture imagery and oversized hero reduce assortment storytelling.

STEP 11 · Too much hero before discovery

Azani phone homepage

Measured phone viewport 388 CSS px. No horizontal overflow was observed. The hero consumes the first screen before age/category discovery.

STEP 12 · Functional; tighten hierarchy

Azani phone collection

Two-column grid works. Title/filter area and help overlay compete with merchandise. Some browser captures include unused margins; these are capture artifacts, not asserted page overflow.

STEP 13 · High-priority usability issue

Azani phone filters

No visible dedicated Close/Show results controls in the captured state. Escape dismissal was verified.

STEP 14 · Usable but tall

Azani phone menu

Matching icons and readable labels. Search is hidden until opening the menu; age links consume a large vertical group.

STEP 15 · Tablet-width evidence only

Kiabi narrower collection

Measured at 919 CSS px; this is not a phone audit. Narrow category/navigation rows continue horizontally.

STEP 16 · Needs intentional recovery

Azani empty Sale

No sale fixtures exist; the generic filter-recovery message does not match the shopper’s intent.

STEP 17 · Clear structure; weak boundaries

Azani sign-in

Simple login choices, pale input borders, long footer. No login, account creation or password reset submitted.

Definition of a strong next version

A parent can find relevant clothing, check size and fit, understand delivery cost, choose an available variant and reach checkout without guessing. The interface stays readable and calm on a phone. Photography communicates quality and age range. Every control has a clear purpose, label and state. Visual consistency supports those tasks.
